Main Focus & Context

In Ephesians 3:1–6, Paul explains how his ministry directly serves God's eternal plan to unite Jews and Gentiles into one new body under Christ[cite: 7, 8]. In chapter 2, Paul established that Gentiles—who were once separated from Christ, alienated from Israel, and strangers to the covenants of promise—have now been brought near by the blood of Christ[cite: 7, 8]. Christ has broken down the dividing wall of hostility and reconciled both groups into a single living temple[cite: 7, 8]. In chapter 3, Paul places this gospel reality in the context of his own apostolic stewardship[cite: 7, 8].

Paul's Stewardship of God's Grace (3:1–2)

Paul introduces himself as a prisoner of Christ Jesus on behalf of the Gentiles[cite: 7, 8]. Though held in Roman custody, he identifies his chains with his divine commission to preach the gospel[cite: 7, 8]. He describes this task as a "stewardship" (oikonomia)—the management or administration of a household[cite: 7, 8]. God's overarching purpose in redemptive history is to sum up all things in Christ (Eph 1:9–10) and build believers into a holy temple (Eph 2:19–22)[cite: 8]. As a faithful estate manager, Paul’s single aim is to testify solemnly to the gospel of God’s grace (Acts 20:24)[cite: 7, 8].

The Mystery Revealed (3:3–5)

The core message Paul manages is "the mystery of Christ"[cite: 7, 8]. In Scripture, a mystery is an end-time truth once partially veiled in the Old Testament but now unveiled in fullness[cite: 7, 8]:

  • Direct Encounter: The mystery was made known to Paul directly on the road to Damascus (Acts 9)[cite: 7, 8]. His three days of physical blindness mirrored his previous spiritual state, after which Christ opened his eyes and commissioned him to go before Gentiles and kings[cite: 7, 8].
  • Progressive Revelation: While God's mercy to the nations was foreshadowed throughout the Old Testament (e.g., Abraham, Rahab, Ruth, Naaman, and the Ninevites), it was not made known in past generations with the clarity now granted to the apostles and prophets by the Spirit[cite: 7, 8]. This was confirmed through Peter’s vision (Acts 10–11), the mission in Antioch, the turning to the Gentiles (Acts 13), and the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15)[cite: 7, 8].

Fellow Heirs, Members, and Partakers (3:6)

Ephesians 3:6 provides a complete reversal of the spiritual alienation of Ephesians 2:12[cite: 7, 8]. Through the gospel, believing Gentiles are now[cite: 7, 8]:

  • Fellow Heirs: Joint inheritors of God's eternal kingdom, whose greatest portion is God Himself[cite: 7, 8].
  • Fellow Members: United into one spiritual body with Jewish believers through the cross[cite: 7, 8].
  • Fellow Partakers of the Promise: Full recipients of every covenant promise in Christ Jesus[cite: 7, 8].

As Galatians 3:26–29 declares, all who are united to Christ by faith belong to Abraham’s seed, receiving the promised rest and citizenship in a heavenly kingdom[cite: 7, 8].
